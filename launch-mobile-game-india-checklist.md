Launching a mobile game in India is less about a single launch day and more about a sequence of decisions made in the right order. Get the order wrong and you spend money acquiring players for a build that does not run well on their phones, or a checkout they cannot use. This checklist is written for studio leads and producers planning an Indian launch, whether India is the first market or the next one.

## 1. Evaluate the market honestly

Start with the right baseline. Lumikai's widely quoted figure of $3.8 billion for FY24 predates the 2025 ban on real-money gaming and included that category. For entertainment games, the relevant figure is the video games market excluding real-money gaming: $1.5 billion in CY25, up 17% year on year, forecast to reach $3.2 billion by CY30.

The regulatory picture is now clearer. The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 received presidential assent on 22 August 2025. It prohibits online money games and their advertising, recognises e-sports, and defines online social games as those played without monetary stakes. It also provides for an Authority on Online Gaming that can categorise and register games and platforms.

- [ ] Use the post-ban market size for revenue planning
- [ ] Confirm the game has no mechanics that could be read as monetary stakes (cash prizes, paid entry with cash-equivalent rewards)
- [ ] Take local legal advice on how the game may be categorised under the Act
- [ ] Decide whether India is a test market, a priority market or a long-term bet

## 2. Check audience fit

India had 591 million gamers in FY24, and the gamer base was 555 million after the ban. About 66% of gamers are outside metros, and the female share of gamers rose from 41% to 44%.

Your target player is probably not the player your team imagines. Pressure-test the assumption early.

- [ ] Define the core player: age band, region, device tier, language
- [ ] Check genre fit against what that player already plays
- [ ] Review whether your art style, themes and session length suit commuting and short-break play
- [ ] Run a small soft test before committing to full localisation

> **Hypothesis to validate:** Session-based games with short, interruptible loops may suit a large share of Indian mobile players. Validate with your own session-length data rather than assuming it.

## 3. Localise for players, not for a checklist

Localisation in India starts with language but goes well beyond it. English reaches many players, but not most of the audience outside metros.

- [ ] Choose launch languages beyond English, and plan a sequence for adding more
- [ ] Review names, festivals, foods, gestures and colours for cultural fit
- [ ] Check UI for text expansion and script rendering
- [ ] Localise store listings, screenshots and ad creatives, not just the game
- [ ] Test with native speakers from the regions you are targeting

## 4. Price for the Indian wallet

Payer conversion held steady at 25% in CY25. Mid-core ARPPU was $15, compared with $3 for casual. Those numbers point to a market where many people will pay, but most will pay small amounts.

Payment method matters as much as price. 83% of players prefer UPI or digital wallets.

- [ ] Set regional price points rather than converting from dollars
- [ ] Offer low-entry starter packs
- [ ] Make UPI and wallet payment paths as short as possible
- [ ] Design a rewarded ad layer for players who will not pay (47% of users are ad-tolerant)
- [ ] Model revenue with both in-app purchases and ads, since IAP is forecast to grow 19% and ads 13%

## 5. Select the right platforms

Android is about 90% of the Indian market, with PC and console at a few percent. Typical smartphones cost $200 to $400.

- [ ] Treat Android as the primary platform
- [ ] Profile performance on low and mid-range devices, not flagship test phones
- [ ] Reduce initial download size and defer optional assets
- [ ] Test on unstable networks and with low storage
- [ ] Decide whether PC or console builds are worth the effort for your genre

## 6. Plan user acquisition

India is the world's second-largest mobile gaming market by downloads, with 15.2 billion game downloads in FY24. Volume is available. The challenge is acquiring players who stay and, eventually, spend.

Gaming is 8% of downloads among India's top 25 apps (16% globally) yet 36% of their revenue (44% globally). Games that break through can monetise well relative to their download share.

- [ ] Set a target cost per retained player, not just cost per install
- [ ] Test creator and community channels alongside paid networks
- [ ] Build creatives in launch languages, with regional variations
- [ ] Use store optimisation for listing text, screenshots and video
- [ ] Hold back budget until soft-launch retention is in a healthy range

> **Hypothesis to validate:** For some genres, community-led and creator-led channels may deliver better retained-player costs than paid networks. Compare cohorts by channel before scaling either.

## 7. Set up analytics before launch

You cannot fix what you cannot see. Instrument the game before the first Indian install, not after.

- [ ] Track funnel steps from install to first session to tutorial completion
- [ ] Segment retention and spend by region, language and device tier
- [ ] Log performance metrics (crashes, load times, frame rate) by device model
- [ ] Track payment attempts and failures by payment method
- [ ] Measure ad impressions and rewarded ad completion separately
- [ ] Agree a short list of launch KPIs with your publishing partner

## 8. Run live operations with a local calendar

Weekly gaming time in India rose 30% to 13 hours. Players who are engaged expect a game that changes with them.

- [ ] Build an events calendar around Indian festivals, holidays and cricket season
- [ ] Plan content updates that work on lighter builds
- [ ] Set up local community channels and a clear support route
- [ ] Review the economy regularly, since small price changes have large effects on low-ARPPU players
- [ ] Consider competitive events, now that e-sports are recognised in law

## A hypothetical example

Consider a hypothetical Korean mid-core RPG studio planning an Indian launch. It might start by profiling the game on a typical $200 to $400 Android phone, cutting the initial download, and adding Hindi alongside English. It would set regional price points, put UPI at the centre of checkout and add rewarded ads for non-payers. It would then soft launch, read retention by device tier and region, and only then scale acquisition. Nothing here is unusual. The discipline is in doing it in order.

## The one-page launch summary

- [ ] Market sized on post-ban figures
- [ ] Legal categorisation reviewed
- [ ] Core player defined and tested
- [ ] Languages chosen and localised content tested
- [ ] Regional pricing and UPI checkout live
- [ ] Android performance profiled on mid-range devices
- [ ] UA plan with retention-based targets
- [ ] Analytics instrumented and KPIs agreed
- [ ] Live ops calendar built around Indian moments
