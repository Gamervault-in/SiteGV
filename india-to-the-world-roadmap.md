Indian studios have never had better tools, talent or access to global storefronts. Yet many strong games still launch abroad with little visibility, weak localisation or commercial terms that do not hold up. Going global is less about one big launch and more about a sequence of good decisions. This roadmap sets out that sequence.

## Why look beyond India

The home market is growing, but its shape matters. Android accounts for about 90% of the Indian market, with PC and console at a few percent, according to Lumikai's FY24 report as reported by GamesBeat. Lumikai's CY25 report, via Outlook Respawn, found that gaming is 36% of revenue among India's top 25 apps, against 44% globally.

For studios building premium PC or console games, or mid-core mobile games with higher revenue per player, international markets are often where most revenue will come from. Many Indian studios will need a global plan from day one, not as a later phase.

## Step 1: Prioritise markets

Trying to launch everywhere at once spreads budget and attention too thin. Start with a short list and a clear reason for each market.

Questions to ask for each candidate market:

- **Genre fit:** Is your genre popular there? Are similar games doing well?
- **Platform fit:** Does the market's platform mix (mobile, PC, console) match your game?
- **Revenue potential:** Will players there pay for your business model, whether premium, free-to-play or ad-supported?
- **Localisation cost:** How much work is needed in language, culture and content?
- **Competition:** How crowded is the genre in that market?
- **Access:** Do you have, or can you build, partners, creators or communities there?

A common pattern is to start with English-speaking markets for testing, then expand into regions where the genre fits best. But the right answer depends on your game.

> **Hypothesis to validate:** Indian studios building casual or hyper-casual mobile games may find Southeast Asia, the Middle East and Latin America better early targets than North America, because user acquisition costs there may be lower relative to player value. Test with small campaigns before committing.

## Step 2: Meet platform requirements

Every platform has its own technical, content and business requirements. Missing them causes delays at the worst possible moment.

### Mobile

- App Store and Google Play policies on data privacy, ads, in-app purchases and loot box disclosures
- Age ratings and regional rules on content and data collection
- Device performance across the range of phones common in each target market

### PC

- Store page quality, trailers and screenshots that meet platform standards
- Wishlists and community building well before launch
- Controller support, achievements and cloud saves where players expect them

### Console

- Developer programme access, dev kits and certification
- Age ratings in each region
- Longer lead times for submission and approval

Plan certification and review time into your schedule. Leaving it to the last month is one of the most common causes of missed launch dates.

## Step 3: Localise properly

Localisation is more than translation. It covers language, culture, user interface and the systems that support players after launch.

- Choose languages based on market priority, not habit
- Build your game so text can expand, fonts can change and right-to-left scripts can work if needed
- Use professional translators who play games, and test translations in context
- Adapt humour, references and names that may not travel well
- Plan community management and support in key languages

Indian studios have a real advantage here. Many already build for a multilingual home market and understand that players respond to games that feel made for them.

## Step 4: Decide on publishing partnerships

Some studios self-publish. Others work with publishers, distributors or regional partners. Neither path is right for everyone.

Self-publishing gives you full control and full revenue, but you carry the cost and work of marketing, platform relationships, localisation, community and analytics.

A publishing partner may help with funding, marketing, platform relationships, localisation, quality assurance and regional expertise. In return, you share revenue and some control.

Before you decide, be clear on:

- What you can do well in-house
- What you would need to hire or buy
- Which markets need local knowledge you do not have
- How much revenue share or control you are willing to give up

Many studios use a mix, self-publishing on some platforms and working with partners in specific regions.

## Step 5: Plan your marketing

Global marketing does not need a global budget, but it does need a plan.

- **Pre-launch:** Build a community through social channels, Discord, wishlists or pre-registration. Share development progress regularly.
- **Creators:** Work with streamers and content creators in each target market. Local creators often matter more than big global names.
- **Events and showcases:** International games events and online showcases can give smaller studios visibility with players, press and partners.
- **Press:** Prepare a clear press kit with trailer, screenshots and key facts.
- **Paid acquisition:** For free-to-play mobile games, test campaigns in each market before scaling, and measure return on spend carefully.
- **Store presence:** Optimise store pages for each market, with localised text and visuals.

## Step 6: Get commercially ready

Many Indian studios underestimate the commercial and legal side of global publishing.

- Set up the right company structure, bank accounts and tax registrations for receiving international revenue
- Understand withholding taxes and how to claim treaty benefits
- Make sure you own or have licensed all code, art, music and other assets
- Register trademarks in key markets
- Comply with data protection rules such as GDPR where relevant
- Have contracts for freelancers and contractors that assign IP to the studio
- Prepare clear financial reporting and forecasts for partners or investors

These steps take time. Start early, ideally months before launch.

## Global readiness checklist

- [ ] Shortlisted priority markets with a clear rationale for each
- [ ] Checked platform policies, age ratings and certification timelines
- [ ] Built the game to support localisation from the start
- [ ] Chosen languages based on market priority and budget
- [ ] Decided between self-publishing, partnerships or a mix
- [ ] Built a pre-launch community and creator plan for each market
- [ ] Prepared a press kit and localised store pages
- [ ] Set up company, banking and tax structures for international revenue
- [ ] Confirmed IP ownership and registered key trademarks
- [ ] Reviewed data protection obligations in target markets
